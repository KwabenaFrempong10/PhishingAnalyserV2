What to Look For

Scammers try to make you act quickly, feel afraid, or hand over information. Here are the main warning signs in plain English.

⏰ Pressure & urgency

Messages that say "act now", "within 24 hours", or "final warning" try to rush you so you don't think clearly.

🚨 Threats

Threats like "your account will be closed" or "you'll lose access" are designed to scare you into acting.

🔑 Asking for secrets

Real companies will never ask for your password, PIN, or verification code in an email.

💳 Money & gift cards

Legitimate organisations don't demand payment by gift card, wire transfer, or cryptocurrency.

🔗 Sneaky links

Shortened links and raw IP-address links can hide where a link really goes. Hover (don't click) to preview.

📎 Attachments & macros

Unexpected attachments — especially programs (.exe) or files asking you to "enable macros" — are dangerous.

🎭 Impersonation

Vague greetings like "Dear Customer" and claims from a fake "security team" are common tricks.

🧾 Surprise invoices

Unexpected invoices or "overdue payment" notes often hide a scam inside the attachment or link.

How the Risk Score Works

There's no mystery number. Every warning sign is worth a set number of points, and the total becomes your score (0–100). Each point is shown and explained in your results.

🟢 Low Risk (0–34)

Few or no warning signs found. Still, always double-check unexpected messages.

🟡 Suspicious (35–69)

Several warning signs. Treat the message with caution and verify it another way.

🔴 High Risk (70–100)

Many strong warning signs. Do not click links or open attachments. Verify officially.

⚠️ Important: This tool can never say for certain whether an email is safe or malicious. It's an educational triage tool that highlights warning signs. A low score doesn't guarantee safety, and a high score doesn't prove harm — but a high score is a strong reason to stop and check.
